PART II · THE PEOPLE WE DECIDED AGAINST

The People We Made Uncomfortable

WE WERE NOT ASKED · Bradley Hobbs

Chapter 6 of 17Reading edition

Sometimes we know we are uncomfortable before we can explain why. Someone speaks differently, dresses differently, has a background unfamiliar to us, or asks a question we were not expecting. We may feel our guard go up. The feeling itself is human; the conclusion we draw from that feeling deserves examination.

Jesus' encounter with Levi in Mark 2:13–171 is a good place to look. He called a tax collector and later ate with people religious observers considered unsuitable company. Their concern centered on whom Jesus was spending time with. Jesus answered by speaking about a physician and those who recognize their need.

Familiarity can become an unwritten rule in church. We may expect everyone to know the language, customs, rhythms, and social cues. A person who does not may be treated as though they are doing something wrong. Sometimes a genuine concern exists. Other times, we have mistaken what feels familiar for what Scripture requires.

We can be honest about uncertainty without making someone else carry the burden of our discomfort. A respectful question, a willingness to listen, or simply a warm greeting can be a better beginning than pretending we already understand. We do not need to make a person's private life a public discussion to offer ordinary kindness.

Conviction and compassion can live in the same conversation. We can say what we believe, remain faithful to Scripture, and still refuse to shame someone. The church should not have to choose between clear teaching and treating people with dignity.

Perhaps the next time we feel uneasy, we can pause before turning that feeling into a judgment. Is there a biblical responsibility here, or am I reacting to something unfamiliar? That question may help us respond more faithfully than our first impulse.

1. Mark 2:13–17 recounts Jesus calling Levi and eating with tax collectors and others considered sinners. Jesus explains His mission through the image of a physician who comes for those who recognize their need.
