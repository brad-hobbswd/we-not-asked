PART III · THE COST OF INTERFERENCE

When Rejection Speaks Louder Than the Gospel

WE WERE NOT ASKED · Bradley Hobbs

Chapter 11 of 17Reading edition

People hear our message through the way we live. A conversation, a social media post, a family interaction, or the way we respond during conflict may become someone's first impression of Christians. We cannot control every impression, but we are responsible for the witness our conduct gives.

Jesus called His followers salt and light in Matthew 5:13–161. The point was not to draw attention to ourselves. Our lives should direct people toward the Father. That becomes difficult when our behavior contradicts the message we claim to carry.

Truth can be communicated without humiliation. A correction can be clear without sarcasm. A disagreement can be honest without contempt. Sometimes the words themselves are accurate, yet our tone or timing turns the conversation into a contest. We should be willing to examine not only what we said, but why and how we said it.

First Corinthians 132 is not merely a passage for weddings. Paul places love in the middle of church life, spiritual gifts, service, and knowledge. The chapter reminds us that being impressive, articulate, or correct is not a substitute for love. Christian witness requires the character of Christ to accompany the message of Christ.

Some people still pray and believe in Jesus while finding church difficult to trust. Others have stepped away after experiences they have not fully explained. We should not assume every question means someone has rejected God. A patient conversation may reveal grief, disappointment, hypocrisy they witnessed, or simply uncertainty.

Listening is not surrendering our convictions. Listening allows us to understand before we answer. When we have caused harm, a specific acknowledgment is more meaningful than a general statement that everyone makes mistakes. Repair takes honesty and changed conduct, not just a sentence of apology.

Online words deserve the same care as spoken words. Ridicule, accusations, and celebrating someone's pain do not become godly communication because a Bible verse is added afterward. James 33 reminds us how powerful speech can be and how easily our words expose contradictions in our lives.

We may not persuade every person, and we cannot guarantee a positive response. Our responsibility is to represent Christ with integrity. When our conduct has obscured the gospel, we should own that and make the changes we can. The message remains true; our witness should become more faithful.

1. Matthew 5:13–16 calls Jesus' followers salt and light, living in a way that directs others toward the Father.

2. 1 Corinthians 13 describes love as essential to Christian service, spiritual gifts, and knowledge, placing Christlike character above outward accomplishment.

3. James 3 warns about the power of speech and the inconsistency of using the tongue both to bless God and harm people made in His image.
