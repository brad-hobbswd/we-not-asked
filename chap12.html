PART III · THE COST OF INTERFERENCE

What Happens When the Invitation Is Interrupted?

WE WERE NOT ASKED · Bradley Hobbs

Chapter 12 of 17Reading edition

Sometimes an encounter with Jesus begins with a question, a need, or an interruption. The person who approaches may not know what to ask. The conversation may not follow the plan we had in mind. We can be so focused on what we expected that we miss the opportunity in front of us.

Acts 8:26–401 tells of Philip meeting an Ethiopian official who was reading Isaiah and trying to understand. Philip did not dismiss the question or assume the man should already know the answer. He joined the conversation, explained the Scripture, and shared the good news about Jesus. The encounter began with listening and led toward understanding.

That is a useful picture of ministry. People often arrive with questions shaped by experiences we do not know. We may need to explain, teach, pray, or simply give someone space to speak. We do not need to treat every question as a challenge to our authority.

Jesus also stopped for people others might have passed by. The Gospels show Him responding to requests, noticing people, and making room for conversations that mattered. His ministry was not reduced to keeping a schedule or protecting His reputation. He was attentive to the person before Him.

Of course, not every interruption can be handled immediately. A leader may be responsible for a group, a safety concern, or another urgent need. The point is not that every request must receive the same response. The point is to avoid making our convenience the measure of someone's worth.

When we cannot help in the moment, we can respond with honesty and kindness. We can explain, offer a next step, or return to the conversation when possible. A person should not have to guess whether they were dismissed because their need was inconvenient.

The invitation to Christ often reaches people through ordinary conversations. We may never know which question opened the door or which patient explanation helped someone keep seeking. Let us make room for those encounters without pretending we are the ones who save. We point toward Jesus; He is the One who gives life.

1. Acts 8:26–40 recounts Philip explaining Scripture to the Ethiopian official, sharing the good news about Jesus, and responding to the man's desire for baptism.
