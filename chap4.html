PART I · THE AUTHORITY WE ASSUMED

Decisions Jesus Never Delegated

WE WERE NOT ASKED · Bradley Hobbs

Chapter 4 of 17Reading edition

After His resurrection, Jesus told His disciples that all authority belonged to Him and then commissioned them to make disciples, baptize, and teach (Matthew 28:18–20). The order leaves no confusion about who holds authority. The work is entrusted to the church, but the authority belongs to Christ.

That does not make ministry passive. We have work to do: proclaim the gospel, teach the Word, care for people, address wrongdoing, and help disciples grow. The question is whether we are carrying out that work or quietly expanding our role until we begin acting as though our approval is necessary for someone to seek God.

Paul described himself and Apollos as servants through whom people believed, while God gave the growth (1 Corinthians 3:5–7). I appreciate how that puts ministry in perspective. We can plant and water. We can encourage and correct. We cannot manufacture spiritual life, and we cannot claim the people God is working in as our own.

Discernment still matters. A church may need to establish boundaries, respond to unsafe behavior, or make a difficult decision. Those responsibilities should be clear, proportionate, and accountable. A decision is not automatically faithful simply because someone with authority made it.

The line becomes easier to see when we ask whether a decision serves a biblical responsibility or protects our personal comfort. Scripture should guide us. Our assumptions should be open to examination. People should be treated with dignity even when a hard conversation is necessary.

Jesus never delegated His place as Savior. He entrusted us with service. That distinction protects both the church and the people we are called to care for.
