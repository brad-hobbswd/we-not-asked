CHAPTER SEVENTEEN Forbid Them Not Mark 10:13–16 records a moment that continues to examine the church. People brought children to Jesus so He might touch them. The disciples rebuked those bringing them, and Jesus became indignant. He told His followers to allow the children to come and not hinder them. He then received the children, laid His hands upon them, and blessed them. The original encounter concerned children, and its meaning must remain grounded in that setting. The correction nevertheless places before every generation a principle worth examining: followers of Jesus must not allow their assumptions to obstruct people whom He is receiving. The disciples were close to the Lord, yet they misunderstood their responsibility. Their correction did not end their discipleship; it became part of their instruction. Jesus confronted their misunderstanding and made certain the children were received. The church must remain willing to learn through correction as well. The responsibility entrusted to us Jesus commissioned His followers to make disciples, baptize, teach, serve, and proclaim the gospel. The mission was never permission to control every person who encountered the message. We are witnesses and servants, stewards of what Christ entrusted, and people who continue to depend upon the mercy of God. Faithfulness requires conviction. We must not become careless concerning doctrine, repentance, obedience, holiness, or spiritual responsibility. The gospel is not ours to rewrite according to cultural pressure or personal preference. Faithfulness also requires humility. We must distinguish teaching Scripture from demanding conformity to assumptions, necessary boundaries from unnecessary barriers, and correction intended to restore from conduct intended to humiliate. The church cannot proclaim Christ’s invitation while behaving as though access to Him belongs to us. The people we have misunderstood Some people may have needed someone to listen without assigning motives, a conversation that did not begin with suspicion, an apology, or acknowledgment that spiritual authority can be misused. We cannot assume every person has experienced such circumstances, and we should not invent stories about someone’s wounds. We must recognize, however, that harm can occur and that our conduct may contribute to it. Listening matters. Acknowledging wrongdoing matters. Examining decisions matters. Recognizing that another person’s experience may differ from our interpretation matters. Changing our conduct when Scripture reveals error matters. A church unwilling to examine itself cannot faithfully claim that every decision reflects the heart of Christ. We must remain willing to learn, repent, make things right where possible, and recognize that people we once misunderstood may have been communicating something we needed to hear. The invitation is greater than discomfort Jesus received people whose presence challenged religious expectations. He spoke with the Samaritan woman, called Matthew, entered Zacchaeus’s home, received the woman who anointed His feet, touched the man with leprosy, sought the man healed of blindness, answered questions, and confronted leaders who placed burdens upon others. His ministry demonstrated that observers did not determine the purpose of His mission. Our discomfort cannot become the final authority over someone’s opportunity to encounter Jesus. Preferences cannot become universal commandments. Assumptions cannot become the measure of someone’s worth. Traditions cannot become more authoritative than Scripture, and positions cannot become excuses for disregarding human dignity. The invitation remains greater than our discomfort. Jesus remains greater than our assumptions. The gospel remains greater than our preferences. Truth must remain truth, and love must remain love This book is not a call to abandon biblical convictions. The church must teach the Word, recognize sin, proclaim repentance, salvation, obedience, and transformation. Scripture also commands love, mercy, humility, patience, forgiveness, restoration, and compassion. We cannot selectively emphasize one responsibility while neglecting another. We cannot defend holiness while disregarding Christ’s character, defend truth while misrepresenting it through our conduct, or defend righteousness while refusing to examine our failures. Truth does not require contempt. Conviction does not require humiliation. Correction does not require cruelty. Holiness does not require dehumanization. Faithfulness does not require pride. The church should teach firmly, love sincerely, correct responsibly, and listen humbly. These responsibilities belong together. When we become the obstacle Sometimes the obstacle is not the person seeking Jesus. It may be our assumptions, traditions, unwillingness to listen, fear of unfamiliar circumstances, misuse of authority, pride, or refusal to acknowledge that we have misunderstood. Such recognition requires courage. Defending a decision may feel easier than examining it. Protecting an institution’s reputation may feel easier than acknowledging someone’s experience. Yet the church cannot faithfully follow Jesus while refusing His correction. When our conduct becomes an obstacle, we must remove the obstacle. When our words wound unnecessarily, we should apologize. When our traditions exceed the authority of Scripture, we must reconsider them. When our decisions fail to reflect compassion, we must change them. When our treatment of people contradicts Christ’s commands, we must repent. The responsibility is not to protect our pride. The responsibility is to represent Jesus faithfully. The people still coming Somewhere, someone is beginning to ask questions about God. Someone is reading Scripture for the first time, considering attending church, praying after years of silence, wondering whether forgiveness is possible, carrying a painful history, trying to understand discipleship, or longing for hope. The church must be ready to receive people without assuming their arrival will be uncomplicated. We must be ready to teach, listen, pray, serve, answer questions, recognize that people need time, correct responsibly, acknowledge failures, and love people who do not resemble us. We must not become so occupied with determining who is acceptable that we forget the people coming. We must not protect preferences at the expense of ministry or become so confident in our understanding that we refuse to learn. Our responsibility is to carry the invitation faithfully. A final examination Have we listened carefully? Have we made assumptions? Have we treated preferences as commandments? Have we allowed traditions to shape our judgments more strongly than Scripture? Have we used authority responsibly, corrected with humility, offered compassion, made room for questions, acknowledged mistakes, and considered whether our conduct made the journey toward Jesus more difficult? These questions should lead toward examination, not hopelessness. The disciples were corrected, and correction became part of their discipleship. We must remain willing to repent and grow. Forbid them not Jesus’ words remain: “Let the little children come to me, and do not forbid or hinder them.” The invitation belongs to Christ. The people seeking Him are not ours to control. The message is not ours to manipulate. The authority is not ours to possess, and the mission is not ours to redefine. We were called to follow Jesus, proclaim the gospel, make disciples, teach His Word, love our neighbors, serve, demonstrate compassion, and remain faithful. We were never called to stand between the Savior and the people seeking Him. The church must be a place where Scripture is taught responsibly, people are treated with dignity, correction serves restoration, leaders remain accountable, questions can be addressed, and the love of Christ is demonstrated through the conduct of His people. The invitation is not ours to own. The Savior is not ours to control. The mercy of God is not ours to limit. Those who need grace must hear the gospel of grace. Those who need truth must receive faithful teaching. Those who need compassion must encounter compassion. We must faithfully carry what Christ entrusted. We were asked to follow Him, proclaim Him, teach His Word, make disciples, love our neighbors, serve, and carry the gospel. We were not asked to stand in His way. Forbid them not.
