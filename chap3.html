PART I · THE AUTHORITY WE ASSUMED

The Disciples Who Became the Obstacle

WE WERE NOT ASKED · Bradley Hobbs

Chapter 3 of 17Reading edition

Mark tells us exactly who Jesus corrected: the disciples. The people bringing children were not asked to explain themselves, and the children were not told to wait until the disciples had settled their disagreement. Jesus addressed the people whose response had created the obstacle.

That detail matters when a church discovers that someone has been hurt by its conduct. We can spend a great deal of time asking why the person reacted as they did, whether they misunderstood, or whether their expectations were reasonable. Those questions may eventually matter. First, we should be willing to examine what we did and hear what the person is trying to tell us.

The disciples may have believed they were acting appropriately. The text does not say. Their intention did not change the fact that Jesus corrected their action. We sometimes use good intentions as the end of a conversation, when intentions should be part of a larger examination that includes truth, effect, and responsibility.

Jesus did not stop at correcting the disciples. He called the children to Himself, received them, and blessed them. The welcome was not theoretical. He made room for the very people who had been turned away.

Matthew 19:13–15 and Luke 18:15–17 also record this encounter. Each account keeps the children at the center of the original scene. We should not pretend the passage directly answers every modern issue. We can, however, ask what the example teaches us about responding when our own assumptions interfere with someone seeking Jesus.1

Being corrected is part of discipleship. We do not have to make the disciples villains to recognize their mistake. We simply have to be willing to learn the same lesson: when Christ shows us that our response is wrong, our next step is obedience, not self-protection.

1. Matthew 19:13–15 and Luke 18:15–17 recount Jesus receiving children after His disciples attempted to prevent people from bringing them to Him. Jesus corrected the disciples, welcomed the children, and taught that the Kingdom of God must be received with childlike humility and trust.
