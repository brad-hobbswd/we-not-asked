ABOUT THE AUTHOR · THE HEART BEHIND THE BOOK

Bradley Hobbs

This book was born from a personal heart cry: the longing to come to Jesus, and the ache of watching other people reach for Him while someone tells them no.

My heart behind We Were Not Asked

There is something deeply personal behind these pages. I know what it means to wrestle with how I approach Jesus, to recognize that I have tried to come to Him through what I understood, what I knew, and the ways I thought I was supposed to. My heart has always been to reach Him.

That longing has shaped the way I look at people. When I watch someone else trying to find their way toward Jesus, asking questions, reaching out, carrying their history, or simply hoping to be welcomed, I cannot ignore the ache of seeing another person step in and tell them no.

Sometimes that no comes from a pulpit. Sometimes from a parent, a spouse, a friend, a church member, or someone who believes they are protecting something. Sometimes it is spoken plainly. Other times it is communicated through judgment, shame, silence, exclusion, or conditions that were never placed there by Jesus.

This book is my cry over that moment: when someone is trying to come to Jesus, and we become the reason they cannot move closer.

“Let the little children come to Me, and do not forbid them; for of such is the kingdom of God.”

MARK 10:14

The rebuke that I cannot overlook

In Mark 10, the disciples thought they had a reason to rebuke those bringing children to Jesus. Jesus did not affirm their decision. He was indignant. He told them to allow the children to come and not to forbid them.

The correction was aimed at the ones standing in the way.

That passage has become more than a familiar moment in Scripture to me. It is a mirror. It forces me to ask whether I have ever become so certain of my role, my expectations, my preferences, or my judgment that I have made the way toward Jesus more difficult for somebody else.

I do not write as someone standing outside the need for correction. I write as someone who must hear the words of Jesus, too. The invitation belongs to Him. The responsibility to follow Him belongs to us. We were never appointed to own the doorway.

Why I wrote this

I watch people reach toward Jesus in the ways they know how. Some come with questions. Some come with wounds. Some are still learning. Some carry things they do not know how to explain. Some are trying again after being pushed away.

My heart aches when the response they receive is a closed door from someone who claims to represent Christ. I believe we must speak truth, live with conviction, protect people, and remain faithful to Scripture. None of those responsibilities gives us permission to treat the seeking person as an interruption to the invitation of Jesus.

This is a call for us to examine ourselves before we decide who else needs to be corrected.

The question I keep returning to

When someone is trying to get to Jesus, am I helping them move toward Him, or have I placed myself in their path?

That question belongs to pastors, leaders, parents, families, church members, and every one of us who has influence over another person's spiritual journey.

A heart cry, not a claim of perfection

We Were Not Asked is not written from a place of having everything figured out. It is an invitation to return to the words and ways of Jesus, to take responsibility for the harm our actions can cause, and to remember that people are not ours to control.

My prayer is that the person who has been told no will hear the invitation of Jesus clearly. My prayer is also that the person who has said no will be willing to hear His rebuke, reconsider, and make room.

May we never become so protective of our own position that we forget the One we are supposed to be following.

We were asked to follow Him.

We were not asked to decide who gets to come.

That is the heart behind this book. That is the burden behind these words. And that is the invitation I hope we will all answer honestly.
