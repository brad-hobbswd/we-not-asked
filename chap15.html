PART IV · RETURNING TO THE HEART OF JESUS

Truth Without Human Obstruction

WE WERE NOT ASKED · Bradley Hobbs

Chapter 15 of 17Reading edition

Truth matters. A church cannot teach faithfully by avoiding Scripture whenever a subject becomes difficult. Yet truth is not a license to speak however we please. The same Bible that calls us to conviction also calls us to humility, patience, love, and careful speech.

Paul instructs believers to speak the truth in love (Ephesians 4:15). The words belong together. Truth without love can become harshness that serves our pride. Love without truth can become avoidance that leaves people without guidance. Christian communication requires both, not as a slogan but as a practice.

Before a difficult conversation, we can ask what the purpose is. Are we trying to help someone understand, protect someone from harm, restore a relationship, or address a real concern? Or are we trying to release frustration, win, embarrass, or prove our authority? Motive does not answer every question, but it can reveal what needs to change in us.

Timing and privacy matter. Some matters should be addressed quietly, with only the people who need to be involved. Public correction may be appropriate in certain circumstances, but it should not be chosen simply because public pressure is easier than a private conversation. The response should fit the situation.

Clarity is kinder than vague insinuation. People should not be left guessing about an expectation or decision. When a concern is legitimate, explain what the concern is, what Scripture or responsibility informs the response, and what steps are possible. When the decision is uncertain, be honest about that too.

Compassion does not remove accountability. A serious concern may require firm action. Even then, dignity remains important. We can set a boundary without insults, correct behavior without humiliating a person, and disagree without treating the other person as an enemy.

Words can be repaired when we are willing to acknowledge them. A specific apology, changed behavior, and openness to further correction carry more weight than a quick defense. We may not be able to erase what someone heard, but we can stop repeating the same harm.

Truth is not weakened by a gentle spirit. The goal is not to make every conversation comfortable. The goal is to speak and act faithfully, so that our manner does not obscure the message we are trying to carry.
