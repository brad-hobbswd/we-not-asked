Reading resources and pathways for engaging with the message of WE WERE NOT ASKED.

When discernment feels difficult

Some men arrive at questions of faith carrying disappointment, unanswered questions, distrust, or experiences that have made certainty difficult. This resource space is meant to offer room to slow down, think carefully, return to Scripture, and seek wise counsel without pressure to manufacture an immediate answer.

Confusion deserves patience. Skepticism can become an honest starting point for careful examination. Discernment grows through prayer, biblical context, humility, truthful conversation, and time.

Practical resources for the journey

RESOURCE 01

Pause Before You Decide

When emotions, experiences, or conflicting voices are loud, take time to name the actual question. Separate what happened, what you were told, what Scripture says, and what remains uncertain.

RESOURCE 02

Read Scripture in Context

Study the passage, its surrounding chapter, audience, setting, and purpose. Avoid building a conclusion around a single phrase detached from the larger witness of Scripture.

RESOURCE 03

Questions Worth Asking

What do I know? What am I assuming? What evidence supports my conclusion? Have I heard the whole story? Am I willing to be corrected? What response reflects truth, humility, and love?

RESOURCE 04

Find Safe, Wise Conversation

Seek mature people who can listen without ridicule, answer honestly, respect boundaries, and remain open to examining Scripture together. Counsel should help you think, not control your conclusions.

Scripture for discernment and steadiness

James 1:5 · Ask God for wisdom.

Acts 17:11 · Examine what you hear against Scripture.

Proverbs 18:13 · Listen before answering.

1 Thessalonians 5:21 · Test and hold fast to what is good.

Romans 12:2 · Let your thinking be renewed.

John 8:32 · Pursue truth with courage.
