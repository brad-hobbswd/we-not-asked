WHEN THE INVITATION IS INTERRUPTED

When We Forbid Them

For the person who has been told no while seeking Jesus, and for every pastor, parent, family member, church member, or leader who must examine whether their response is helping someone come to Christ or standing in the way.

“Let the little children come to Me, and do not forbid them; for of such is the kingdom of God.”

Mark 10:14

Jesus did not merely welcome them. He rebuked those who tried to stop them.

People brought children to Jesus, hoping He would touch and bless them. The disciples rebuked those bringing the children. The disciples may have believed they were protecting Jesus' time, managing the crowd, or deciding who deserved access. Whatever their reasoning, their actions placed an obstacle between people and Jesus.

Mark records that Jesus was indignant. His response was direct: allow them to come, and do not forbid them. The correction was not directed toward the people seeking Jesus. It was directed toward the disciples who had tried to stop them.

The passage asks a sobering question of anyone who holds influence in a church, family, ministry, or community: Have I become the person who makes coming to Jesus harder?

The disciples' mistake can happen again

We do not need to stand physically in front of someone to obstruct their approach to Christ. Our words, expectations, reactions, traditions, shame, control, or refusal to listen can become a barrier.

01 · PASTORS & LEADERS

When access becomes conditional

A pastor or ministry leader may unintentionally communicate that a person must first look right, speak right, know the right people, have the right history, or resolve every struggle before being welcomed to seek Jesus. Leadership is entrusted with care, teaching, and protection. Leadership is not permission to make the invitation of Christ our personal property.

Examine: Are our expectations guiding people toward Jesus, or making our approval feel like a prerequisite for approaching Him?

02 · FAMILY MEMBERS

When love becomes control

Family members can discourage spiritual seeking through fear, criticism, pressure, old labels, arguments, or demands that someone conform before conversations can happen. Concern may be sincere, yet the way concern is expressed can shut the door instead of keeping a relationship open.

Examine: Am I listening to the person who is seeking, or am I using family influence to dictate how, when, or whether they may pursue faith?

03 · CHURCH MEMBERS

When familiarity creates a gate

Church members may become gatekeepers through gossip, suspicion, coldness, social exclusion, unwelcome comments, or treating a visitor as an interruption. Someone may be welcomed from the platform yet quietly discouraged in the hallway, foyer, classroom, or fellowship circle.

Examine: Does my presence make a newcomer feel there is room to approach Jesus, or that they must earn a place among us first?

04 · ANYONE WITH INFLUENCE

When our preferences become obstacles

We can hinder people by confusing personal preference with biblical command, protecting our comfort, assuming the worst, demanding a polished testimony, or deciding someone is too complicated, disruptive, or different to belong near us while seeking Christ.

Examine: Am I defending something Jesus commanded, or something I simply prefer?

Ways we can become the disciples in the passage

  • We dismiss people before hearing them. Their questions, history, appearance, or reputation become enough for us to decide they do not belong.
  • We make access depend on our approval. We communicate that a person must satisfy us before they can be welcomed or taken seriously.
  • We use shame as a correction tool. We humiliate, expose, mock, or scold someone whose next step may require gentleness and honest conversation.
  • We mistake inconvenience for a spiritual problem. Their presence, questions, timing, or needs interrupt our plans, so we treat the interruption as something to eliminate.
  • We protect our image more carefully than a person's pursuit of God. We become concerned about what others will think and less concerned about the person in front of us.
  • We assume we know what Jesus would do without listening to what He said. The disciples acted, but Jesus corrected them.

For the person who has been told no

Being discouraged, rejected, shamed, or turned away by people does not mean Jesus has rejected you. The disciples' rebuke was not Jesus' invitation. In this passage, Jesus calls the ones being blocked toward Himself.

Your questions do not have to be hidden to be brought honestly before God. Your seeking does not become invalid because someone responded poorly. You may need wise counsel, boundaries, time, and a safe place to process what happened. Do not confuse a human barrier with the heart of Christ.

Read the passage for yourself. Notice who rebuked whom. Notice who Jesus called forward. Notice whose actions displeased Him. Let Jesus' own words be heard above the voices that pushed you away.

What a Christ-centered response can look like

Listen before correcting

Ask what the person is seeking, what happened, and what they need to understand. Do not presume the whole story from a first impression.

Make room without abandoning wisdom

Welcoming someone is not the same as ignoring safeguarding, accountability, healthy boundaries, or biblical teaching. Boundaries should protect people, not serve as excuses for contempt or exclusion.

Point toward Jesus, not yourself

Help people encounter Scripture, prayer, genuine community, and Christ. Do not make your approval, status, or personal preferences the center of their spiritual journey.

Repair the harm when necessary

When you realize your words or actions obstructed someone, acknowledge the harm without defensiveness, apologize honestly, and change your approach.

Read the passages together

Mark 10:13–16 · Jesus is indignant when the disciples block the children, tells them not to hinder them, then welcomes, embraces, and blesses them.

Matthew 19:13–15 · The disciples rebuke those bringing children. Jesus directly says not to forbid them from coming to Him.

Luke 18:15–17 · Jesus calls the children to Himself after the disciples rebuke those bringing them.

Mark 9:38–40 · The disciples report trying to stop someone acting in Jesus' name because the person was not following their group. Jesus tells them not to stop him.

Luke 9:49–50 · A similar account shows Jesus correcting the disciples' impulse to forbid someone because he was not among their company.

Questions for personal and ministry examination

  1. Who have I discouraged from seeking Jesus, intentionally or unintentionally?
  2. Have I confused my preferences, traditions, comfort, or reputation with the commands of Christ?
  3. How do we treat people who arrive with difficult questions, visible struggles, unfamiliar backgrounds, or painful histories?
  4. Would someone who has been turned away feel safe telling me what happened?
  5. Do my words and actions resemble the disciples' rebuke, or Jesus' welcome?
  6. What needs to change so the people seeking Jesus are not unnecessarily hindered by me?

We were asked to follow Jesus.

We were not asked to control who gets to approach Him. The warning of this passage is not only for those who were turned away. The rebuke belongs to those who did the turning away.
