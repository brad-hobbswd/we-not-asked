PART I · THE AUTHORITY WE ASSUMED

When We Mistook Proximity for Authority

WE WERE NOT ASKED · Bradley Hobbs

Chapter 2 of 17Reading edition

Being close to someone does not mean we understand every decision they would make. The disciples had walked with Jesus, heard His teaching, and seen His ministry. Still, they misread a moment and needed correction. Proximity had not made their judgment infallible.

Church service can have the same effect on us. Years of involvement, sacrifice, and responsibility may give us valuable perspective. Those things can also make us confident that our preferences should carry more weight than someone else's questions. We may start confusing familiarity with authority.

Peter gives a useful reminder in 1 Peter 5:2–3.1 Elders are told to shepherd willingly and not domineer over those entrusted to them. The responsibility is real, but the posture is service. Leadership is not ownership of people's consciences, families, or relationship with God.

Questions can be handled poorly on either side. Some are asked with hostility, and some decisions must be made even when everyone will not agree. Yet a sincere question deserves an honest response. We can explain, return to Scripture, seek counsel, or admit that we need to reconsider. A title should never replace the work of giving a reason.

Healthy spiritual leadership helps people become mature, discerning followers of Christ. People should not have to depend on one person's approval to understand Scripture or grow in faith. Respect and accountability belong together, and leaders remain learners under the same Word they teach.

The disciples had responsibility, but Jesus was still Lord over the moment. That is a good boundary for all of us. Our service matters, but our confidence must stay submitted to Christ.

1. 1 Peter 5:2–3 instructs elders to shepherd God's people willingly and faithfully, serving as examples rather than exercising domineering control. The passage presents spiritual leadership as stewardship and service, not ownership.
