CONCLUSION The Invitation Was Never Ours to Control Some words remain with us long after the conversation has ended. Jesus’ instruction in Mark 10:14 (AMPC) is among them. The scene was brief: people brought children to Him, the disciples tried to prevent the approach, and Jesus corrected them. He received the children and blessed them. The encounter reveals a boundary His followers must remember. We are called to serve Christ, but we are not given ownership of the people He receives. Throughout these pages, we have considered the assumptions that can shape religious decisions, the histories used to define people, the discomfort that can become distance, the wounds caused by spiritual authority, and the consequences when those who were meant to serve become obstacles. None of these subjects should remain an accusation aimed only at someone else. The final examination belongs to each of us. We can sincerely believe that we are protecting the church and still need correction. We can know Scripture, serve faithfully, and carry responsibilities while misunderstanding the people standing before us. We can defend an established practice without considering whether the practice reflects the command of Christ or a preference that has gradually acquired the weight of authority. The disciples' correction reminds us that sincerity and proximity do not make our judgments infallible. The purpose of this book has never been to diminish Scripture, excuse sin, or remove the responsibility of discipleship. Jesus calls people to Himself, and He calls those who follow Him to repentance, obedience, and transformation. The church must teach that message faithfully. Our responsibility is also to examine whether personal pride, fear, tradition, or discomfort has added burdens that Scripture does not require or caused us to treat people without the dignity Christ commands. Truth and compassion cannot be separated without damaging our witness. Teaching must be faithful, correction must be responsible, and accountability must protect those who need protection. None of those responsibilities requires ridicule, humiliation, careless assumptions, or the reduction of a person to a label or former circumstance. A person is more than the subject of our disagreement. A person is more than the history we know, and more than the conclusion we have reached before listening. The church must be willing to hear those who describe painful experiences, while also approaching every circumstance with discernment and fairness. Listening does not mean accepting every account without examination. It means refusing to dismiss a concern simply because hearing the concern challenges our preferred explanation. Where we have caused harm, we must acknowledge the harm, take responsibility, and make changes appropriate to the circumstances. An apology should not become a demand that someone immediately trust us again. We must also remember that discipleship is not a process we control. We teach, encourage, correct, pray, and serve, but the work of conviction and transformation belongs to God. Our role is not to manipulate another person's response or make our approval the condition of beginning a spiritual journey. We are called to faithfully carry the gospel and remain available to help people learn the way of Christ. The questions raised by this book are therefore not answered by a final defense of our intentions. They are answered through the choices we make after hearing the words of Jesus. Will we examine the authority we exercise? Will we reconsider a tradition when Scripture requires us to do so? Will we listen when someone tells us our conduct has caused harm? Will we correct without humiliating, protect without degrading, and hold conviction without contempt? Will we welcome correction ourselves? The disciples were corrected, and the correction was part of their instruction. Their failure did not become the authority for future generations. The words of Jesus did. We must be willing to learn where we have misunderstood, repent where we have been wrong, and change where faithfulness requires change. Such humility does not weaken the church. It places the church again under the authority of Christ. When someone comes seeking Jesus, may they encounter people who are prepared to proclaim the gospel, answer questions honestly, offer prayer, teach Scripture, and demonstrate compassion. May our congregations be places where truth is spoken with integrity, leaders remain accountable, people are not exposed unnecessarily, and correction seeks restoration rather than spectacle. May our ministries serve those entrusted to us instead of protecting positions for their own sake. The invitation belongs to Christ. His message is ours to carry, not rewrite. His authority is ours to honor, not imitate as though we possessed it without limit. His church is ours to serve, not own. The people who come seeking Him are not ours to control. Jesus' words still require our attention: “Let the little children come to Me, and do not forbid or hinder them.” May we hear the correction before someone else bears the cost of our misunderstanding. May our words reflect the Savior, our conduct reflect the gospel, our hearts remain teachable, and our hands remain ready to serve. We were asked to follow Him, proclaim Him, teach His Word, make disciples, love our neighbors, and carry the gospel. We were not asked to stand in His way. Amen.
