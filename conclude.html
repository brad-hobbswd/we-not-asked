CONCLUSION The Invitation Was Never Ours to Control Jesus’ words in Mark 10:14 remain before us: “Let the little children come to Me, and do not forbid or hinder them” (Mark 10:14, AMPC). The original encounter concerned children being brought to Him, and the disciples were corrected when they attempted to prevent the approach. Their correction reminds us that closeness to Jesus does not guarantee that every assumption or decision reflects His heart. Throughout these pages, we have examined spiritual authority, inherited assumptions, judgment, exclusion, religious wounds, and the responsibility of the church to represent Christ faithfully. The purpose was not to establish ourselves as judges over those who have failed. The purpose was to examine those entrusted with carrying the gospel. The distinction between following Jesus and attempting to manage the people around Him matters. Following Him requires obedience, humility, love, service, patience, and willingness to receive correction. Control can lead us to defend preferences, protect positions, and exercise authority beyond what He entrusted. The church was called to carry the message, not own the invitation. Our conduct must demonstrate that the gospel has shaped our hearts. Truth must remain central to our faith, and love must remain central to our conduct. Neither responsibility permits neglect of the other. The people we encounter are not interruptions to ministry. They are human beings whom we are called to love. Their circumstances may require discernment, teaching, patience, and prayer, but their humanity must never become secondary to our desire to defend ourselves. We must remain willing to examine our conduct. When people experience our ministries, do they encounter compassion? When someone asks a difficult question, do we respond with patience? When someone has been wounded, do we listen before defending ourselves? When someone needs correction, do we seek restoration? When someone approaches Jesus, do we help them draw nearer? The disciples were corrected, and their correction became part of their instruction. Acknowledging our failures does not diminish the gospel. Repentance demonstrates our willingness to submit our conduct to Christ. The church becomes more faithful through humility. Compassion does not remove our responsibility to teach Scripture, and recognizing the dignity of people whose experiences differ from ours does not require abandoning conviction. Our responsibility is to represent Jesus. The invitation belongs to Christ. The message belongs to Christ. The authority belongs to Christ. The church belongs to Christ, and the people seeking Him belong to God. Our calling is to follow, proclaim, teach, serve, love, and faithfully carry the gospel. When someone comes seeking Jesus, may they encounter people willing to listen, teach, pray, serve, and recognize that grace was never theirs to ration. May our lives never contradict the message we proclaim. May our churches never mistake unnecessary barriers for faithfulness. May our ministries remain more concerned with serving people than protecting our positions. May our words reflect the Savior, our conduct reflect the gospel, our hearts remain teachable, and our hands remain ready to serve. May our faithfulness never become an excuse for forgetting compassion. The words of Jesus remain our responsibility to hear. Forbid them not. We were asked to follow Him, carry His message, love, serve, make disciples, and proclaim the gospel. We were not asked to stand in His way. May we never forget the difference. Amen.
