CONCLUSION The Invitation Was Never Ours to Control Some words remain with us long after the conversation has ended. Some passages of Scripture continue to examine our hearts long after we have finished reading. Jesus’ words in Mark 10:14 (AMPC) should remain among those words. The moment was brief, yet the lesson reaches into every generation of believers. People were seeking Jesus, and those who had been called to follow Him were preventing the encounter. The disciples needed correction. Their understanding of responsibility had become confused, and Jesus made certain the people they had attempted to hinder were received. The story leaves no room for us to assume that proximity to Jesus automatically makes every decision consistent with His heart. We can know Scripture and still misunderstand the people standing before us. We can participate in ministry and still misuse the authority entrusted to us. We can sincerely believe that we are protecting the church while creating unnecessary barriers for those seeking God. We can defend traditions so vigorously that we fail to recognize when those traditions have become more influential than the teachings of Jesus. Throughout these pages, we have examined the ways religious assumptions can become obstacles, the consequences of judgment without humility, the wounds created by spiritual rejection, and the responsibility of the church to represent Christ faithfully. We have considered the people who were made uncomfortable, those whose histories were used against them, those who stopped coming, and those who may still be wondering whether the invitation of Jesus remains available to them. The purpose was never to establish ourselves as judges over those who have failed. The purpose was to examine the responsibility of those entrusted with carrying the gospel. Every believer must eventually confront the difference between following Jesus and attempting to manage the people around Him. Following Jesus requires humility, obedience, love, service, patience, and willingness to receive correction. Attempting to manage others often requires us to defend preferences, protect positions, and exercise authority beyond what Christ entrusted. That responsibility requires more than knowing what we believe. Our conduct must demonstrate that the gospel we proclaim has shaped our hearts. The manner in which we treat people matters. Our willingness to listen matters. Our humility when corrected matters. Our compassion toward those carrying pain matters. Our recognition of the dignity of every human being matters. Truth must remain central to our faith, and love must remain central to our conduct. Neither responsibility gives us permission to neglect the other. The people we encounter are not interruptions to ministry. They are people whom we have been called to love. Their circumstances may require wisdom, discernment, teaching, patience, and prayer, yet their humanity must never become secondary to our desire to defend ourselves. When people experience our ministries, do they encounter the compassion of Christ? When someone approaches Jesus, do we help them draw nearer, or do our assumptions create another obstacle? These questions belong to every congregation, ministry, leader, and believer. None of us should consider ourselves beyond examination. The disciples were corrected by Jesus, and their correction became part of their instruction. We must remain teachable in the same way. Acknowledging where we have failed does not diminish the gospel. Repentance demonstrates our willingness to submit our conduct to Christ. The church does not lose its responsibility to teach Scripture when compassion becomes visible. The church demonstrates the character of Christ when truth is carried with humility and love. Recognizing the dignity of people whose experiences differ from ours does not require abandoning conviction. The church fulfills its responsibility when people are treated as human beings rather than reduced to labels, histories, assumptions, or arguments. There will always be people who need the gospel, instruction, encouragement, correction, restoration, prayer, and compassion. May we never become so concerned with controlling those opportunities that we forget the mission entrusted to us. May we never become so confident in our own understanding that we refuse to hear the correction of Jesus. May we never allow preferences to become the measure of another person’s worth or pride to become an obstacle to those we are called to serve. May we never mistake standing beside Jesus for possessing the authority to stand between Jesus and someone seeking Him. Our calling is to follow, proclaim, teach, serve, love, and faithfully carry the gospel into the world. When someone comes seeking Jesus, may they encounter a church that understands its responsibility, people willing to listen, teach, pray, and serve, and believers who understand that grace was never theirs to ration. May our churches never become places where unnecessary barriers are mistaken for faithfulness. May our ministries never become more concerned with protecting positions than serving the people Christ loves. The invitation belongs to Christ. The message belongs to Christ. The authority belongs to Christ. The church belongs to Christ, and the people seeking Him belong to God. Our calling is to follow, proclaim, teach, serve, love, and faithfully carry the gospel. When someone comes seeking Jesus, may they encounter people willing to listen, teach, pray, serve, and recognize that grace was never theirs to ration. May our lives never contradict the message we proclaim. May our churches never mistake unnecessary barriers for faithfulness. May our ministries remain more concerned with serving people than protecting positions. May our words reflect the Savior, our conduct reflect the gospel, our hearts remain teachable, and our hands remain ready to serve. The words of Jesus remain our responsibility to hear. Forbid them not. We were asked to follow Him, carry His message, love, serve, make disciples, and proclaim the gospel. We were not asked to stand in His way. May we never forget the difference. Amen.
