Conclusion The Invitation Was Never Ours to Control Some words remain with us long after the conversation has ended. Some passages of Scripture continue to examine our hearts long after we have finished reading. Jesus’ words in Mark 10:14 should remain among those words. “Let the little children come to me, and do not forbid or hinder them.” The moment was brief, yet the lesson reaches into every generation of believers. People were seeking Jesus, and those who had been called to follow Him were preventing the encounter. The disciples needed correction. Their understanding of responsibility had become confused, and Jesus made certain that the people they had attempted to hinder were received. The story leaves no room for us to assume that our proximity to Jesus automatically makes every decision we make consistent with His heart. We can know Scripture and still misunderstand the people standing before us. We can participate in ministry and still misuse the authority entrusted to us. We can sincerely believe that we are protecting the church while creating unnecessary barriers for those who are seeking God. We can defend our traditions so vigorously that we fail to recognize when our traditions have become more influential than the teachings of Jesus. The possibility should humble us. Throughout these pages, we have examined the ways religious assumptions can become obstacles, the consequences of judgment without humility, the wounds created by spiritual rejection, and the responsibility of the church to represent Christ faithfully. We have considered the people who were made uncomfortable, the people whose histories were used against them, the people who stopped coming, and the people who may still be wondering whether the invitation of Jesus remains available to them. The purpose was never to establish ourselves as judges over those who have failed. The purpose was to examine the responsibility of those who have been entrusted with the gospel. Every believer must eventually confront the difference between following Jesus and attempting to manage the people around Him. The distinction matters. Following Jesus requires humility, obedience, love, service, patience, and a willingness to receive correction. Attempting to manage the people around Him often requires us to defend our preferences, protect our positions, and exercise authority beyond what Christ entrusted to us. The church was never called to own the invitation. The church was called to carry the message. That responsibility requires more than knowing what we believe. Our conduct must demonstrate that the gospel we proclaim has shaped our hearts. The manner in which we treat people matters. Our willingness to listen matters. Our humility when corrected matters. Our compassion toward those carrying pain matters. Our recognition of the dignity of every human being matters. Truth must remain central to our faith, and love must remain central to our conduct. Neither responsibility gives us permission to neglect the other. The people we encounter are not interruptions to our ministry. They are people whom we have been called to love. Their circumstances may require wisdom, discernment, teaching, patience, and prayer, yet their humanity must never become secondary to our desire to defend ourselves. The church must remain willing to examine the fruit of its conduct. When people experience our ministries, do they encounter the compassion of Christ? When someone asks a difficult question, do we respond with wisdom and patience? When someone has been wounded, do we listen before defending ourselves? When someone disagrees with us, do we preserve their dignity? When someone needs correction, do we seek restoration? When someone approaches Jesus, do we help them draw nearer, or do our assumptions create another obstacle? These questions belong to every congregation, every ministry, every leader, and every believer. None of us should consider ourselves beyond examination. The disciples were corrected by Jesus, and their correction became part of their instruction. We must remain teachable in the same way. Acknowledging where we have failed does not diminish the gospel. Repentance demonstrates our willingness to submit our conduct to the authority of Christ. The church does not become weaker through humility. The church becomes more faithful. The church does not lose its responsibility to teach Scripture when compassion becomes visible. The church demonstrates the character of Christ when truth is carried with humility and love. The church does not surrender its convictions by recognizing the dignity of people whose experiences differ from its own. The church fulfills its responsibility when people are treated as human beings rather than reduced to labels, histories, assumptions, or arguments. Jesus remains the Savior. Jesus remains the Shepherd. Jesus remains the One who calls. Jesus remains the One who receives. Jesus remains the One who forgives. Jesus remains the One who transforms. Jesus remains the One whose authority exceeds our own. Our responsibility is to faithfully represent Him. There will always be people who need the gospel. There will always be people who need instruction. There will always be people who need encouragement, correction, restoration, prayer, and compassion. There will always be opportunities to demonstrate the love of Christ through our words and actions. May we never become so concerned with controlling those opportunities that we forget the mission entrusted to us. May we never become so confident in our own understanding that we refuse to hear the correction of Jesus. May we never allow our traditions to overshadow Scripture. May we never allow our preferences to become the measure of another person’s worth. May we never allow our pride to become an obstacle to the people we are called to serve. May we never mistake standing beside Jesus for possessing the authority to stand between Jesus and someone seeking Him. The invitation belongs to Christ. The message belongs to Christ. The authority belongs to Christ. The church belongs to Christ. The people seeking Him belong to God. Our calling is to follow, proclaim, teach, serve, love, and faithfully carry the gospel into the world. When someone comes seeking Jesus, may they encounter a church that understands its responsibility. May they find people who are willing to listen, people who are willing to teach, people who are willing to pray, people who are willing to serve, and people who understand that grace was never theirs to ration. May our lives never become a contradiction to the message we proclaim. May our churches never become places where unnecessary barriers are mistaken for faithfulness. May our ministries never become more concerned with protecting our positions than serving the people Christ loves. May our words reflect the Savior. May our conduct reflect the gospel. May our hearts remain teachable. May our hands remain ready to serve. May our doors remain open to those seeking the truth. May our faithfulness never become an excuse for forgetting compassion. The words of Jesus remain our responsibility to hear. Forbid them not. We were asked to follow Him. We were asked to carry His message. We were asked to love. We were asked to serve. We were asked to make disciples. We were asked to proclaim the gospel. We were not asked to stand in His way. May we never forget the difference. Amen.
