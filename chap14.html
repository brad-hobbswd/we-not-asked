PART IV · RETURNING TO THE HEART OF JESUS

He Never Told Them to Leave

WE WERE NOT ASKED · Bradley Hobbs

Chapter 14 of 17Reading edition

The Gospels show Jesus meeting people in circumstances that made others uncomfortable. He spoke with people, touched those considered unclean, stopped for those calling out, and welcomed conversations that revealed both need and faith. The stories are not all alike, and we should let each one retain its details rather than flatten them into a slogan.

In Mark 1:40–45,1 a man with leprosy approached Jesus and asked to be made clean. Jesus was moved with compassion, touched him, and healed him. The touch matters in the story. Jesus did not keep the man at a distance while offering a response from a safe place. He met the request directly.

The passage also shows that Jesus gave instructions following the healing. Compassion did not mean the encounter lacked direction. The man received healing and was told what to do next. The Gospel holds together the tenderness of Jesus and His authority.

Other encounters remind us that Jesus noticed people others overlooked. The blind man calling out, the woman reaching for His garment, and Zacchaeus in the tree each appear in a different setting.2 Their stories are not interchangeable. What they share is that Jesus engaged the person rather than allowing the crowd's reaction to determine the value of the encounter.

Church ministry should be careful not to turn people into illustrations. We can learn from biblical accounts without using someone else's pain or private story as a sermon prop. People deserve to be heard in their own right, not merely because their circumstances make a point.

Sometimes we will not know what help is needed until we ask. We may need to pray, explain Scripture, offer practical support, or make a referral. We should avoid promises we cannot keep and respect the limits of our role. Compassion is not pretending to have every answer.

Jesus remains the center of these encounters. Our responsibility is not to recreate every circumstance, but to follow His example of attentiveness, truth, and care. A person should leave our conversation knowing they were treated as a human being, even when the conversation included challenge or correction.

1. Mark 1:40–45 tells of Jesus compassionately healing a man with leprosy and giving him instructions afterward.

2. The encounters referenced include the blind man near Jericho (Mark 10:46–52), the woman who touched Jesus' garment (Mark 5:25–34), and Zacchaeus (Luke 19:1–10). Each account is distinct, showing Jesus responding to people in their particular circumstances.
