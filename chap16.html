CHAPTER SIXTEEN We Were Never the Gatekeepers The church has a responsibility to teach Scripture, make disciples, exercise discernment, and care for people entrusted to its ministry. Those responsibilities are real, but they do not make the church the owner of Christ’s invitation. We are stewards of what He entrusted, not gatekeepers claiming authority beyond His Word. Christ remains the Shepherd John 10 presents Jesus as the Good Shepherd who knows His sheep, calls them, protects them, and lays down His life for them. The church serves under that Shepherd; we do not replace Him. Spiritual leadership includes teaching, guidance, protection, correction, and accountability. Its purpose is to help people follow Christ faithfully, not claim possession of their spiritual lives. The church should examine whether its decisions guide people toward Scripture and Jesus or make personal approval the center of their experience. The authority entrusted to believers must remain accountable to the Lord. The keys do not make us owners Matthew 16 records Jesus speaking to Peter concerning the keys of the kingdom and the authority connected with binding and loosing. The passage must be understood within the authority of Christ and the responsibilities He entrusted. It does not establish independent authority exercised apart from Him. Leadership cannot use a passage concerning authority as protection from examination or permission to elevate personal judgment beyond Scripture. The church serves the kingdom. The kingdom does not belong to us. The Savior remains the One who calls Hebrews 4:14–16 directs believers toward Jesus, the great High Priest, and encourages them to approach the throne of grace with confidence to receive mercy and help. The invitation is grounded in Christ’s work and compassion. The church should direct people toward that access rather than make itself the center of the encounter. Believers can pray, teach Scripture, offer guidance, and provide pastoral care. Those responsibilities serve the purpose of helping people approach the Lord. The church cannot become the source of mercy, and human approval cannot replace Christ’s invitation. The Spirit guides toward truth John 16 records Jesus teaching concerning the work of the Holy Spirit, including guidance into truth and the glorification of Christ. The Spirit’s work is not a license for personal claims that cannot be examined. Believers must remain accountable to Scripture and humble in their understanding. A congregation should not use spiritual language to make personal preferences unquestionable. Claims of divine direction must be handled carefully, and leadership remains accountable to the Word. People can sincerely misunderstand and require correction. Servant leadership Matthew 20:25–28 contrasts worldly authority with the servant posture Jesus required of His followers. Greatness among His disciples is expressed through service. The Son of Man came not to be served but to serve and give His life. This establishes the character of Christian leadership. Authority is not a means of elevating oneself. Leadership should be measured through faithfulness, humility, service, and care for people. A leader who demands unquestioned control has departed from the posture Jesus described. Those entrusted with influence remain servants of Christ and His people. Stewards, not owners The Great Commission in Matthew 28:18–20 begins with Jesus’ authority and continues with the mission He entrusted to His followers. We are commanded to make disciples, baptize, and teach people to observe His commandments. The commission is substantial, but the authority belongs to Christ. Stewardship requires faithfulness. We must teach Scripture, exercise discernment, address conduct responsibly, and care for people. We must not add requirements Scripture does not establish or treat personal approval as a condition for approaching Jesus. Necessary boundaries may serve safety, accountability, and responsible ministry. Such boundaries should be grounded in clear purpose and Scripture, not used to preserve comfort or protect a position from examination. The church must recognize the difference between faithful stewardship and controlling the invitation. The responsibility belongs to us We are responsible for how we teach, lead, correct, and treat people. We must examine our assumptions, receive correction, and remain accountable. We are responsible for carrying the gospel faithfully. We are not the Savior. We are not the source of grace. We are not owners of the people Christ calls. We are servants entrusted with His message. The church must remain a community where Scripture is taught faithfully, leaders are accountable, correction serves restoration, people are treated with dignity, and the invitation of Jesus is not obscured by unnecessary human barriers. The authority belongs to Christ. The mission is His. The people are His. We were never the gatekeepers of His invitation. We were called to faithfully carry it.
