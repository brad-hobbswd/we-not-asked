PART IV · RETURNING TO THE HEART OF JESUS

We Were Never the Gatekeepers

WE WERE NOT ASKED · Bradley Hobbs

Chapter 16 of 17Reading edition

Most churches need people who organize, teach, protect, make decisions, and keep ministry moving. Responsibility is not the problem. The problem begins when we forget what responsibility is for and start treating the people or ministry entrusted to us as though they belong to us.

A steward manages something that belongs to another and remains accountable for the work. That is a helpful way to think about church leadership. We may be trusted with a class, a ministry, a schedule, a building, or a decision. We are not the owner of the church, and our role does not make our preferences unquestionable.

Boundaries can be necessary. Safeguarding, accountability, and clear expectations protect people and make ministry possible. A boundary should have a real purpose, be explained where appropriate, and be applied fairly. A barrier created mainly to avoid discomfort or preserve someone's status is a different matter.

Romans 141 reminds believers not to despise or judge one another over the matters of conscience discussed in that chapter. The passage does not make every issue optional. It does warn us against turning personal conclusions into contempt for another believer. We should be careful to distinguish what Scripture clearly requires from the customs or preferences we have adopted.

Decisions should be communicated respectfully. When a matter affects someone directly, they deserve to understand the concern and have an appropriate opportunity to ask questions. Privacy must be protected. We should never make someone else's circumstances public merely to defend our decision.

Leaders also need accountability. A decision may be based on incomplete information, an inconsistent standard, or a misunderstanding. Reviewing a decision does not undermine authority. It demonstrates that authority remains submitted to Christ and Scripture. Those who lead should be able to seek counsel, admit mistakes, and make corrections.

Jesus described leadership as service in Matthew 20:25–282. He did not teach His followers to dominate people to establish greatness. Service means considering those affected, explaining when possible, protecting the vulnerable, and accepting responsibility for the consequences of our choices.

Stewardship should make us more careful, not more entitled. We have been entrusted with ministry so that people may be served and Christ honored. When a boundary is necessary, carry it with humility. When a barrier is unnecessary, be willing to remove it.

1. Romans 14 addresses disputes over matters of conscience and warns believers against contempt and judgment, reminding each person to answer to God.

2. Matthew 20:25–28 contrasts worldly domination with Jesus' model of servant leadership, culminating in His own example of giving His life for others.
